Every fire, 1992-2020

Each dot is one fire's point of origin. Colour shows the cause, the size, or the protected-area status of the land it started on. The list beside the map counts what is shown, so the picture always comes with its n.

How to read it

  • Dots overlap. Where they pile up the colour saturates, so a dense area looks the same whether it holds a thousand fires or ten thousand. Tap or click the map for the actual count around a point.
  • The map starts with the 322,769 fires of 10 acres or more. Add the 1,980,796 smaller fires with the button; most of them are in the South and East.
  • Some states did not report small fires in some years (see coverage). A year where a state looks empty may be a year its records are missing.
  • "Not in PAD-US" means the point falls outside every protected or public area in the Protected Areas Database; most private land is in that group.

What is encoded

Each fire is 8 bytes: its projected position and a 32-bit word with the year, day of year, cause class, size class, protection status, state and general cause. ESRI:102003 lower 48; EPSG:3338 x0.35 Alaska inset; ESRI:102007 Hawaii inset; EPSG:32161 Puerto Rico inset. Built by scripts/build_points.py from the same parquet file as every other number here; 1 fire falls outside the map frame and is not drawn.

Protection status comes from a point-in-polygon join to PAD-US 4.1 (details). Size classes follow the FPA FOD: A under 0.25 acres, B 0.25-10, C 10-100, D 100-300, E 300-1,000, F 1,000-5,000, G 5,000 and more.

Data: FPA FOD 6th edition (Short 2022), SHA-256 04f5ab8b (full hash in docs/DATA_VERSION.md).
