Methods and ledger

Every number on this site is a claim in a JSON ledger produced by the analysis code, with its definition, the number of records behind it and the time it was computed. The site is generated from that ledger; nothing is typed in by hand except the prose.

What it isA compilation of federal, state and local wildfire reports: one row per reported fire with a discovery date, a final size and a point location at least as precise as a PLSS section. Nonfederal records were not available from every state for every year, so counts underrepresent activity in some places and periods.
What it is notNo perimeters (points only), no prescribed fire, no burn severity, no structures or losses, no weather or fuels.

Glossary

Regions
West = AZ CA CO ID MT NV NM OR UT WA WY; South = AL AR FL GA KY LA MS NC OK SC TN TX VA WV; Northeast = CT ME MA NH RI VT NY NJ PA; Alaska = AK; Hawaii/PR = HI PR; Other = every other STATE value (Midwest, Plains, DC, DE, MD). ()
Size classes
FIRE_SIZE_CLASS as coded in FPA FOD: A < 0.26 acres, B 0.26-9.9, C 10-99.9, D 100-299, E 300-999, F 1,000-4,999, G 5,000 and up.
Large fire
On this site, "large" means at least 300 acres (Classes E, F and G), the size at which ICS-209 incident reporting becomes common. The trend claim uses Class G (5,000+ acres) because those fires are captured by every reporting system. MTBS maps burn severity only for fires of at least 1,000 acres in the West and 500 acres in the East, so severity data exists for a subset of "large" fires.
Ownership classes
OWNER_DESCR as recorded at the ignition point, upper-cased so the two spellings of "Private" merge: BIA, BLM, BOR, COUNTY, FOREIGN, FWS, MISSING/NOT SPECIFIED, MUNICIPAL/LOCAL, NPS, OTHER FEDERAL, PRIVATE, STATE, STATE OR PRIVATE, TRIBAL, UNDEFINED FEDERAL, USFS. Ownership at the ignition point is not ownership of the area burned.
Missing rules
The explicit categories "Missing data/not specified/undetermined" (cause) and "MISSING/NOT SPECIFIED" (owner) are counted as missing; those fields have no nulls. Missing categories are always shown. "Human share of known cause" always excludes the undetermined category from the denominator and says so. Zero-record state-years are reporting gaps, not fire-free years.
Month
DISCOVERY_MONTH, the month of the discovery date: a fire counts in the month it was found, not the months it burned.
Containment time
DURATION_HOURS = CONT_DATETIME minus DISCOVERY_DATETIME, defined only when both a date and a valid HHMM time exist; DURATION_DAYS = CONT_DATE minus DISCOVERY_DATE in whole days. Both measure a containment declaration under the reporting system's convention, not response speed.
Trend tests
Kendall tau and the Theil-Sen slope on 29 annual points, plain p-values with no serial-correlation adjustment; the 95% interval is the Theil-Sen slope interval.
1-degree cells
1-degree cells: cell_lat/cell_lon = floor(LATITUDE)/floor(LONGITUDE) (south-west corner); n_fires; acres = sum FIRE_SIZE; n_human, n_natural, n_missing_cause from NWCG_CAUSE_CLASSIFICATION; human_share_known = n_human / (n_human + n_natural); human_share_all = n_human / n_fires
GACC approximation
State-level approximation of the ten NWCG GACCs; whole states assigned to one area, California North/South merged, Idaho and Wyoming not split. Lists: Alaska = AK; Northwest = OR WA; California (North+South merged) = CA; Great Basin (approx) = ID NV UT; Northern Rockies (approx) = MT ND; Rocky Mountain (approx) = CO WY SD NE KS; Southwest = AZ NM; Southern Area (approx) = AL AR FL GA KY LA MS NC OK SC TN TX VA PR; Eastern Area (approx) = CT DE DC IA IL IN ME MD MA MI MN MO NH NJ NY OH PA RI VT WV WI; Hawaii (not a GACC) = HI Only Northwest (OR WA), Southwest (AZ NM), Great Basin members (ID NV UT WY) and the ten GACC names were verified from gacc.nifc.gov pages fetched 2026-09-25; the rest is unverified.

Errata

Claims from the earlier version of this project that did not survive the check, kept next to what the check found.

A June spike in Class G acreage in Arkansas

What was claimed: A June spike in Class G acreage in Arkansas, in the earlier README.

What the check found: Refuted: Arkansas has 2 Class G fires in 29 years and none in June. June is the peak Class G month for Arizona and Alaska; the README most likely mislabels AZ or AK as AR.

n = 4,783 · · evidence claims: anom.arkansas_class_g anom.june_class_g_by_state_top10

December fires in Texas as a recurring pattern

What was claimed: December fires in Texas as a recurring pattern, in the earlier README.

What the check found: Not a general pattern: December ranks 10 of 12 Texas months by fires and 10 of 12 by acres. Texas's big-fire season is January to April. One December stands out: 2005 with 94,523 acres, 2.7x the next largest December.

n = 180,087 · · evidence claims: anom.texas_by_month anom.texas_december_top_year

A 2010 outlier in the Northeast

What was claimed: A 2010 outlier in the Northeast, in the earlier README.

What the check found: Not reproduced with this region definition: 2010 has z = 0.98 on fires and z = 0.48 on acres, ranking 7 of 29 years by fires. The series is dominated by reporting shifts (largest jumps in 2020, 2005, 2015) and by states with zero-record years.

n = 180,427 · · evidence claims: anom.northeast_2010 anom.northeast_largest_yoy_count_jumps anom.northeast_zero_report_years

"Higher highs and higher lows" for Class G acres

What was claimed: the Class G acreage trend has higher highs and higher lows.

What the check found: the upward trend holds (Kendall tau ), but 2010 ( acres) is the fifth-lowest of 29 years, so the lows are not rising.

n = 4,783 ·

Classes B and C are "most often" debris burning

What the check found: debris and open burning is the most common single general cause in B () and C (), a plurality and not a majority; in Class C arson is within 1.2 points.

The Control Efficiency Score ranks agencies on response

What the check found: the ranking flips between the mean-based and median-based versions (Spearman ); the score follows final fire size and reporting convention. Retired; see Ownership.

What the check found: the Random Forest reproduces exactly but loses to predicting zero days on mean absolute error (1.38 against 1.09 days); see Prediction.
