Time to a containment declaration

Read this before the chart. The containment fields record when a reporting system declared the fire contained, under that system's own convention. They do not measure response speed, and they are missing for of all records, unevenly by owner: of BLM records have a containment date, of records with no recorded owner. A Class A fire is under a quarter of an acre wherever it burns, yet the USFS median is hours and the unrecorded-owner median hours: that is a difference in what "contained" means to each system, not in the fire.

For the fires from 2010 on with both a discovery and a containment date-time, the median time to the containment declaration is hours (75th percentile , 90th , 99th hours); are recorded as zero hours.

The Control Efficiency Score was retired

The earlier version of this project ranked owners with a Control Efficiency Score, defined as 1 divided by the mean acres burned per hour to containment. It was retired for two reasons that the data shows directly:

  • The ranking is an artefact of the aggregation. With the mean, USFS ranks of 14 owners; with the median it ranks . BLM moves from to , Private from to . The Spearman correlation between the two rankings is : they are unrelated.
  • The score mostly measures final fire size. Median acres per hour rise from in Class A to in Class G, so an owner's score follows the size mix of its fires and the containment convention of its reporting system, not its response.

Neither version measures response quality, and no agency or owner ranking on speed or efficiency appears on this site. The chart below is kept as the record of why.
