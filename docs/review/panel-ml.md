# Panel review: ML methodology (Part 2, fire-duration Random Forest)

**Reviewer role:** ML methodologist, adversarial. **Date:** 2026-09-25.
**Data:** `data/fires.parquet` (same rows as `FPA_FOD_20221014.sqlite`, hash in `docs/DATA_VERSION.md`).
**Scratch code:** `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-wildfire-analysis/a4df9529-3e52-5fbc-b7e0-fb427368a57f/scratchpad/panel-ml/` (`common.py`, `run1_temporal.py`, `run2_spatial.py`, `run3_geo_only.py`, `run4_reframe.py`, `run5_doy366.py`, `run6_alternatives.py`; logs `run*.log`). All fits use `n_jobs=1`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`. The model sample is rebuilt from the parquet exactly as `src/features.preprocess_data` builds it (2010+, `CONT_DATE` present, whole-day duration, negatives dropped, sin/cos of DOY/365, 13 cause dummies, lat, lon); `common.py` documents this.

Definitions used throughout: "RF" is the README configuration (100 trees, max depth 10, seed 42). "Predict-zero" and "predict-train-mean" are constants fitted on the training rows. "Climatology" is the training-set mean (or median) duration by 1-degree latitude/longitude cell and calendar month, requiring at least 20 training fires, backing off to the cell and then to the global value. "LARGE" is `FIRE_SIZE >= 300` acres (NWCG classes E, F, G). Subsamples are drawn with `random_state=42`.

## (a) Verdict

The duration model should not survive in its current form. Its headline gain (RMSE 9% better than the mean) is an artefact of the random split: on a forward-in-time test (train 2010-2017, test 2018-2020) the gain falls to 3.0%, and in 2018 alone the RF loses to a constant. On the metric that describes the typical fire (MAE) it is 26% worse than predicting zero on every split I ran. Eighty-three percent of its squared error comes from the 1% of fires lasting 30+ days, which it predicts at about 9 days. Lat/lon alone recovers 83% of its skill; adding cause dummies makes it worse. The target is whole days on a sample that silently drops 26% of fires, including 88% of Texas. The README's "what it can't do" section is honest about long fires but wrong about short ones. A defensible replacement is a calibrated probability of a rare outcome (fire runs past day 0, or past 7 days, or exceeds 300 acres) evaluated forward in time against a cell-by-month climatology, which the exploratory run shows does carry usable skill.

## (c) Should the duration model exist, and what should practitioners get instead?

**Short answer: not as a day-count regressor.** A number of days with RMSE 8 on a target whose 84% mode is 0 and whose 99th percentile is 24 is not a quantity anyone can act on, and every honest comparison in (b) shows it does not beat trivial rules on the typical fire. What a dispatcher or a pre-positioning planner can use is a calibrated probability that a newly reported fire will be one of the rare escalating ones, ranked against the background rate for that place and month. That is a classification problem with a climatology baseline, and it can use all 854,880 fires from 2010+ because `FIRE_SIZE` is never missing.

**Exploratory reframe (`run4_reframe.py`; not tuned, not a final result, no holdout was locked).** Train 2010-2018 (400k-row subsample), test 2019-2020 (all rows). Climatology = Laplace-smoothed P(outcome) by 1-degree cell and month from training years, backing off to cell then global. "Brier skill" = 1 - Brier/Brier(base rate).

Operational readings from the same run: for LARGE on all fires, the top 10% of HGB scores contain 52.6% of the large fires at a 5.3x lift over the base rate (climatology: 46.4%, 4.6x). For P(duration >= 7 days) the top decile holds 59.7% of positives at 6.0x lift. For P(duration > 0) the top decile has an observed rate of 64.6% against a 16.5% base. HGB calibration by quantile bin is monotone and close to the diagonal for all four targets (e.g. LARGE, all fires: top bin predicted 0.080, observed 0.073).

What this says:

1. **The signal is real but it is climatology.** For LARGE, the cell-by-month lookup gets ROC-AUC 0.81 and the best learner 0.85; lat/lon alone beats the full feature set on PR-AUC and Brier. The README's features add almost nothing beyond "where and when". Any replacement must report its gain over climatology, not over a coin flip.
2. **Brier skill on LARGE is 3 to 5%.** The ranking is useful (half the large fires sit in the top decile), the probabilities are barely sharper than the base rate. That is the honest headline for a size-based early-warning claim with these inputs.
3. **The hurdle target P(duration > 0) has the most skill (Brier skill 0.30, PR-AUC 0.59 vs 0.17 base)**, but it inherits the CONT_DATE selection and the midnight-crossing artefact (finding 8), so it is measuring "will this fire be logged as contained on a later calendar day", which is partly a reporting behaviour. P(duration >= 7 days) is the more meaningful duration outcome and still shows Brier skill 0.17.
4. **Recommended target for the replacement piece:** P(LARGE) on all 2010+ fires as the primary (no selection, operationally meaningful, base rate 1.4%), with P(duration >= 7 days) as a secondary on the CONT_DATE sample with its coverage caveat stated. Present both as ranked risk with calibration plots and lift at the top decile, always alongside the climatology baseline, and say plainly that without fuel and weather inputs the model is a learned map of where large fires happen.

## (d) Proposed evaluation protocol for the replacement

1. **Locked holdout.** Fires discovered in 2019-2020 (136,819 rows for the all-fires sample) are the test set. Define it in `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md` before any further modelling and touch it once, at the end. Everything in (c) above was run on those years and therefore counts as a spent look; the lead should note that in the log and either accept it as the single exploratory look or move the holdout to 2020 only.
2. **Temporal CV for development.** Expanding-window folds: train 2010-2014 test 2015; train 2010-2015 test 2016; train 2010-2016 test 2017; train 2010-2017 test 2018. Report the mean and the per-year range; a model that wins on average but loses in a year is reported as such (finding 7).
3. **Spatial block CV as a secondary check.** GroupKFold by 1-degree cell (and, as a harder variant, by state) on the development years, to show how much skill is transferable to unseen places versus memorised.
4. **Baselines, all fitted on training years only:** base rate; climatology by cell x month (smoothed as in `run4`); logistic regression on the same features; the geography-only learner. Every metric table includes all of them.
5. **Metrics:** PR-AUC (primary, because the base rate is 1 to 4%), ROC-AUC, Brier score and Brier skill against base rate and against climatology, top-decile lift and recall, and a reliability diagram with 10 quantile bins. If a duration regressor is retained anywhere, report MAE and RMSE against predict-zero, predict-mean and cell-by-month median, on hours not days, plus per-bucket error.
6. **Calibration.** Apply isotonic or Platt calibration on the last development fold only if the raw reliability diagram is off; report before and after.
7. **Tuning.** Small grid on HGB (learning rate, max leaf nodes, l2, min samples per leaf) using the temporal folds; log every configuration in `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`, including the losing ones.
8. **Importance.** Grouped permutation importance on the holdout (geography, season, cause), with confidence intervals from repeats. No MDI.
9. **Reporting rules.** Sample definition and coverage by state in the same table as the metrics. Any sentence of the form "the model can X" must point to the metric that shows it.
