# WFIGS 2021-2025: a second, separate reporting system

**Produced by:** `python -m analysis.wfigs --stage all --out outputs/` (about 4.5 minutes total: 2.3 minutes to
download and clean, 1.8 minutes to analyze and forward-test). **Outputs:** `outputs/claims_wfigs.json` (18
claims, same schema as `outputs/claims.json`, ids prefixed `wfigs.`), `data/external/wfigs/wfigs_2021_2025.parquet`,
`data/external/wfigs/download_manifest.json`, `data/external/wfigs/layer_schema.json`, 98 raw gzip JSON pages
under `data/external/wfigs/raw_pages/`, and the forward-test model at
`outputs/model/reduced_g1_hgb.joblib` (not committed; rebuilt by `python -m analysis.wfigs --stage analyze`). Every number below is a claim id in
`claims_wfigs.json`; the site should render from that file, not from this text.

The site's per-fire data is FPA FOD, which stops at FIRE_YEAR 2020. This adds WFIGS (Wildland Fire Interagency
Geospatial Services) incident locations for fires discovered 2021-01-01 to 2025-12-31, as its own panel. WFIGS
is never merged into the FPA FOD series: the two systems count fires on different rules (section 2 below), so
splicing WFIGS onto the 1992-2020 trend would silently change what "number of fires" means partway through the
chart.

## 1. Source

**Service.** `https://services3.arcgis.com/T4QMspbfLg3qTGWY/arcgis/rest/services/WFIGS_Incident_Locations/FeatureServer/0`,
layer 0, "Incidents". Found from the ArcGIS Online item search for "WFIGS Incident Locations" and the NIFC Open
Data site (data-nifc.opendata.arcgis.com), and confirmed by reading the layer's own schema (`?f=json`) on
2026-09-26: 97 fields, `maxRecordCount` 2,000, point geometry. NIFC publishes three related layers; this is the
**full-history** one, confirmed by an unfiltered count of 426,988 rows with `FireDiscoveryDateTime` back to about
2003. The other two (`WFIGS_Incident_Locations_YearToDate`, current calendar year only, and
`WFIGS_Incident_Locations_Current`, open incidents only) would both silently drop most of 2021-2024.

**Query.** `IncidentTypeCategory = 'WF' AND FireDiscoveryDateTime >= TIMESTAMP '2021-01-01 00:00:00' AND
FireDiscoveryDateTime < TIMESTAMP '2026-01-01 00:00:00'`, `outSR=4326`, `returnGeometry=true`, paged with
`resultOffset`/`resultRecordCount=2000` and `orderByFields=OBJECTID` for a stable order. `IncidentTypeCategory`
has three values on this layer: `WF` (wildfire), `RX` (prescribed fire) and `CX` (a complex record grouping
several incidents); only `WF` is a wildfire discovery, so `RX` and `CX` are excluded. Fields kept: `OBJECTID`,
`UniqueFireIdentifier`, `IrwinID`, `IncidentName`, `FireDiscoveryDateTime`, `IncidentSize`, `FinalAcres`,
`DiscoveryAcres`, `FireCause`, `FireCauseGeneral`, `POOState`, `POOLandownerCategory`, `POOLandownerKind`,
`POOProtectingAgency`, `InitialLatitude`, `InitialLongitude`, `IsFSAssisted`, `ContainmentDateTime`,
`ControlDateTime`, `FireOutDateTime`, `IncidentTypeCategory`, `CreatedOnDateTime_dt`, `ModifiedOnDateTime_dt`,
plus geometry x/y.

**Rows and access.** 195,139 raw rows over 98 pages, accessed 2026-09-26 (UTC). By year: 2021 36,908; 2022
37,816; 2023 37,165; 2024 41,516; 2025 41,734 (`wfigs.rows_fetched_raw`, `wfigs.query`). The count query and the
paged fetch agreed exactly, so no rows were added or changed mid-pull (WFIGS is live operational data, so this
is not guaranteed on a slower pull). SHA-256 of the cleaned parquet:
`158ef4c817fe6b6716103856e32e75b1110b7201842d8b4fcdf02ff05d08d4d1` (`wfigs.parquet_sha256`).

## 2. Cleaning

One row per fire, deduped on `UniqueFireIdentifier` (46 rows dropped, keeping the one with the largest
`OBJECTID`, i.e. the most recently synced version). No rows were dropped for missing location or missing
discovery date: 33,328 rows had no `InitialLatitude`/`InitialLongitude` but did have point geometry, and those
were used to recover the coordinate instead of dropping the row (`wfigs.cleaning`).

**Size field.** WFIGS carries three acreage fields. Coverage over the 195,093 cleaned rows: `IncidentSize`
152,300 (78.1%), `DiscoveryAcres` (used only when `IncidentSize` is null) 33,253 (17.0%), `FinalAcres` (used
only when both are null) 2,364 (1.2%), none of the three 7,176 (3.7%). `size_acres` takes `IncidentSize` first
-- it is the incident's most recently reported acreage as tracked through IRWIN, the closest analog to FPA
FOD's `FIRE_SIZE` -- falling back to `FinalAcres` (only present on an approved final fire report, so sparse but
authoritative) and then `DiscoveryAcres` (the size at first report, which understates any fire that grew) to
recover as many rows as possible. 187,917 of 195,093 rows (96.3%) end up with a size.

**Cause.** `FireCause` has five values: `Human`, `Natural`, `Undetermined`, `Unknown`, and null. Mapped to
Human / Natural / Missing, with `Undetermined`, `Unknown` and null all folding into Missing, to match the
three-way split `analysis.large_fire` already uses for FPA FOD's `NWCG_CAUSE_CLASSIFICATION`.

**Owner.** `POOLandownerCategory` (17 values: ANCSA, BIA, BLM, BOR, City, County, DOD, DOE, Foreign, NPS,
OthFed, OthLoc, Private, State, Tribal, USFS, USFWS, plus null) mapped to the same six classes
`analysis.large_fire.OWNER_MAP` uses for FPA FOD's `OWNER_DESCR`: Federal = USFS BLM NPS USFWS BOR BIA DOD DOE
OthFed; State = State; Private = Private; Tribal = Tribal, ANCSA (Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act
corporation land has no FPA FOD equivalent, and is grouped with Tribal as the closest available category); Other
= City County OthLoc Foreign; Missing = null.

**State and region.** `state` strips the `US-` prefix from `POOState` (e.g. `US-CA` to `CA`); `region` is
`analysis.common.region_of` on that code, the same five-plus-one scheme used everywhere else in this package.

## 4. Is WFIGS comparable to FPA FOD? (`wfigs.comparability_verdict`)

FPA FOD 2016-2020 (all states) is used as the comparison panel, computed with the identical measures
(`wfigs.comparison_fpa_fod_2016_2020`):

| Measure | FPA FOD 2016-2020 | WFIGS 2021-2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Mean fires/year | 76,390 | 39,019 (51% as many) |
| Missing-cause share | 9.7% | 49.7% |
| Human-cause share | 80.1% | 36.4% |
| Natural-cause share | 10.2% | 13.9% |
| Share of fires >= 300 acres | 1.3-1.8% by year | 2.2-3.0% by year |

**Verdict, in plain terms.** WFIGS counts about half as many fires a year as FPA FOD. That is a scope
difference, not a WFIGS data-quality problem: FPA FOD compiles reports from federal, state, and many local and
volunteer fire departments, so it captures a large number of very small (Class A and B) fires that local
agencies log but that never enter the interagency IRWIN system WFIGS is built on. WFIGS looks close to complete
for fires that get a federal or state incident record, and its 300-acre-or-larger share is consistently higher
than FPA FOD's for the same kind of year, which is what you would expect if WFIGS is undercounting small fires
relative to FPA FOD rather than missing large ones.

The missing-cause share is five times higher in WFIGS (49.7% vs 9.7%). The site should not read this as WFIGS
reporting cause worse than FPA FOD; a fire's cause on an incident record can still be under investigation, and
WFIGS reflects a different, more federal/state-weighted mix of reporting agencies than FPA FOD's broader roll-up.

**Conclusion for the site:** show WFIGS 2021-2025 as its own recent-years panel (fires, acres, cause mix,
regional pattern, the 300-acre share), but never splice it onto the FPA FOD 1992-2020 series as if it were the
next five years of the same count. The denominators are not comparable.

## 5. Forward test: a reduced-feature version of the large-fire model

The site's large-fire model (`analysis/large_fire.py`, `outputs/claims_model.json`) predicts P(final size >=
300 acres) at discovery using 96 features, most of them from the FPA FOD-Attributes join (weather, fuels,
terrain, protection status) that has no WFIGS equivalent. This section trains a small model on only the
features both datasets can supply, and checks how it ranks WFIGS fires it has never seen.

**Setup.** Train: FPA FOD 2010-2020, CONUS only (AK, HI, PR excluded), 842,239 fires. Test: WFIGS 2021-2025,
CONUS only, rows with a size, 185,618 fires. Features, built identically on both sides: latitude, longitude,
leap-aware sin/cos of day of year, cause class (Human/Natural/Missing, mapped as in section 2), owner class
(Federal/State/Private/Tribal/Other/Missing, mapped as in section 2), and month. Label: final size >= 300
acres. Model: `HistGradientBoostingClassifier(learning_rate=0.05, max_leaf_nodes=63, min_samples_leaf=50,
l2_regularization=1.0, max_iter=500, early_stopping=True, random_state=42)`, `categorical_features='from_dtype'`
on cause class and owner class. Baseline: the same cell x month climatology lookup `analysis.large_fire` uses
(1-degree cell, Laplace-smoothed toward the cell rate and then the global rate with k=20 pseudo-counts),
copied verbatim into `analysis/wfigs.py` and fit on the same FPA FOD 2010-2020 CONUS training rows. Both are
evaluated, untouched, on WFIGS 2021-2025.

**Results, overall (185,618 fires, 4,486 >= 300 acres):**

| Scorer | PR-AUC [95% CI, 1,000 resamples] | ROC-AUC | Top-10%-by-rank capture | Brier skill vs train base rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lookup: cell x month climatology | 0.081 [0.075, 0.086] | 0.734 | 34.1% | +0.025 |
| Reduced HGB model (7 features) | 0.041 [0.039, 0.044] | 0.655 | 21.2% | -0.257 |

Base rate: 2.42% of WFIGS test fires reach 300 acres, against 1.37% in the FPA FOD 2010-2020 training data.

**Per year** (PR-AUC, lookup vs model):

| Year | n | n >= 300 ac | Lookup PR-AUC | Model PR-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 35,113 | 877 | 0.073 | 0.043 |
| 2022 | 34,983 | 947 | 0.095 | 0.048 |
| 2023 | 35,299 | 758 | 0.075 | 0.036 |
| 2024 | 39,899 | 1,040 | 0.088 | 0.041 |
| 2025 | 40,324 | 864 | 0.075 | 0.042 |

The lookup beats the reduced model in every single year, by roughly the same margin each time.

**Reading this result.** On the full model's own FPA FOD 2019-2020 holdout, the full 96-feature model beats
the same cell x month lookup by a wide margin (PR-AUC 0.139 vs 0.075, see `docs/findings/large_fire_model.md`).
Here, with only seven features and a different reporting system on the test side, the relationship flips: the
reduced model loses to the lookup, consistently, across all five years.

### 5a. Why it failed: a post hoc diagnostic (`wfigs.forward_test_ablation`)

This check was added by the lead reviewer *after* seeing the result above, so it explains the failure; it is not
a clean forward test of a new model. The same model settings were refitted with three feature sets and scored
two ways: forward in time inside the FPA FOD (fit 2010-2018, score 2019-2020) and across systems (fit FPA FOD
2010-2020, score WFIGS 2021-2025 CONUS).

| Inputs | PR-AUC, FPA FOD 2019-2020 | PR-AUC, WFIGS 2021-2025 [95%] | WFIGS top-10% capture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Location + season | 0.086 | 0.111 [0.104, 0.119] | 40.5% |
| + cause class | 0.091 | 0.107 | 40.5% |
| + cause + owner class (pre-specified) | 0.091 | 0.041 | 21.2% |
| Lookup: cell x month | 0.075 | 0.081 [0.075, 0.086] | 34.1% |

Inside the FPA FOD the owner feature helps slightly. Across systems it is what breaks the model. 41.3% of FPA FOD
2010-2020 training fires have no owner recorded, and only 0.63% of those reached 300 acres: they come mostly from
local reporting systems that record many small fires. WFIGS records an owner for all but 0.05% of fires, and 65%
of them are coded Private (32% in the FPA FOD training data). The owner rule the model learned describes FPA FOD
reporting conventions, not fires, and it does not carry over. Cause class is missing for half of WFIGS fires
but does little harm. Without owner, the location-and-season model beats the lookup on 2021-2025 fires.

This is the model card's warning made concrete. The full model also leans on owner, agency and protection
inputs that partly encode reporting systems, which is one more reason the site shows its scores as ranks only
and does not claim it would transfer to another system unchanged.

**What the site should say and not say** (`wfigs.forward_test_note`). This reduced model is a lower bound on
what the full model would do on new discoveries, not a test of the full model itself: it is missing the
weather, fuels, terrain and protection-status features that the full model's own ablation shows account for
most of its skill, and it is being scored on a different reporting system than it was trained on. The two
PR-AUC numbers (0.139 on the full model's own holdout, 0.041 for this reduced model on WFIGS) should never be
shown side by side as if they measured the same thing. What can be said plainly: a small model built from only
location, season, cause and owner does not automatically rank new fires well when moved to a different
reporting system, and here a simple spatial lookup did better than the pre-specified model (section 5a shows why). The reduced model is saved at
`outputs/model/reduced_g1_hgb.joblib` (not committed; rebuilt by `python -m analysis.wfigs --stage analyze`) for anyone who wants to inspect or rerun it;
it is not the model the site's Prediction page should use for anything beyond this forward test.

## 6. What the site can and cannot say with WFIGS

**Can say:**
- WFIGS fire counts, acres, cause mix, regional pattern, and the 300-acre share, for 2021-2025, as their own
  panel with its own axis label ("WFIGS incident locations, 2021-2025", not "FPA FOD, continued").
- The prevention calendar (month x cause) for the current five years.
- Per-state counts and the human-share-of-known-cause for 2021-2025.
- That a simple lookup of place and month outranks a small model built only from location, season, cause and
  owner when that model is moved from FPA FOD to WFIGS, a real, checked result, and that the post hoc
  diagnostic traces the failure to the owner field (section 5a).

**Cannot say:**
- That WFIGS 2021-2025 continues the FPA FOD 1992-2020 trend lines. It does not use the same intake, and
  showing them on one continuous series would understate 2021-2025 fire counts by roughly half.
- That the large-fire model's published 0.139 PR-AUC applies to WFIGS-era fires. That number comes from 96
  features on FPA FOD; nothing tested here reaches it.
- That WFIGS's higher missing-cause share means worse reporting than FPA FOD. It reflects a different agency
  mix and, for open incidents, a cause that may not yet be finalized.
- Anything about Alaska, Hawaii or Puerto Rico from the forward test: it is CONUS-only, for the same reason the
  full large-fire model is CONUS-only (the training side has no non-CONUS attributes to match against).
